# C2 editorial policy acceptance scenarios

These manual application scenarios record the user's confirmed decisions. Evaluate them against the complete transcript context; do not treat the examples as an exhaustive phrase list.

1. **Product comment is not customer pain.** “这件是我们苔藓绿的 M 码大货 / 对这种颜色呢，它比较怕流水印子” describes the product/color. It is not a customer concern and must not open as a “困扰” hook.
2. **A direct customer question can be pain.** A quoted viewer comment such as “小个子穿这件会不会显矮？” is a valid customer-pain candidate. Keep the complete question and follow it with the related fit explanation; the host's answer alone is not the hook.
3. **Pain is a candidate, not a fixed priority.** Compare appearance, core-selling-point, and customer-pain candidates by actual appeal; do not force a category order. In apparel, an attractive “好看” line can beat a weaker pain, while a strong pain with related follow-up may still open or serve as another requested plan's hook. If no related explanation exists, use another opening and retain a clear, useful pain statement later when it is a distinct customer need; state that the source has no matching explanation and do not imply the product solves it.
4. **Choose a product core claim over sizing advice.** If the candidates are “158穿短款” and supported product-specific claims such as “新国标九五的鹅”“面料是做三防的工艺”, use the core product selling point as the opening. Keep height/size advice in the later selection section.
5. **Appearance wording and color-only wording differ.** A complete “穿起来很好看” or “显瘦又好看” can open. Color matching, “显白” alone, or “显年轻” alone cannot. A line combining an eligible appearance benefit with color wording may remain whole.
6. **Customer source matters.** A clear viewer comment or host paraphrase of a customer concern can be a pain candidate. “主播喜欢这件” alone does not represent audience appeal; a complete original statement that explains a strong appearance reason may be considered as a whole. Buyer feedback may open when the source is clear; sales counts, repeat-purchase figures, and bare purchase statements belong later.
7. **Use only necessary opening context.** If “这个/它” needs context, include the shortest necessary original lead-in. If the context is long, find a shorter self-contained hook or use the core selling point. Never repair the hook with invented words.
8. **Match pain to the next section.** A fit/height concern should lead into the related fit explanation and its original length/waist details; finish that fit topic before switching to material. Do not answer it with unrelated material copy or invent a solution.
9. **Material platform notice applies at sentence scope.** Locate the platform-identified word or exact sentence. If a flagged word occurs in a sentence, remove that complete sentence rather than cutting just the word. In a subtitle containing multiple independent sentences, keep clean sentences; if the whole cue is the flagged sentence, remove that cue. Retain adjacent clean subtitles and do not remove the paragraph or all material discussion.
10. **Same-sentence useful detail follows the flagged sentence.** If a material word and useful detail are in the same sentence, remove that sentence as a whole. If the same cue has a separate clean sentence, retain it with a precise note that the flagged sentence is cut and the cut point still needs source-audio/video review. Do not reproduce the flagged sentence in the clip, title, or tags.
11. **No spillover from one material statement.** A platform flag on “白鹅绒” does not automatically ban “九五的鹅” or an independently stated three-proof process. Assess each exact statement separately. Missing proof without a platform finding is a verification note, not automatic removal.
12. **Suspected transcription is scoped.** If the SRT likely misrecognizes a material word, mark it as suspected and remove only the affected sentence/cue as a precaution where context clearly supports the match. Do not call a suspicion a confirmed platform finding or remove the whole paragraph.
13. **Styling advice follows the product description.** Record all distinct outfit pairings and their conditions in the highlight inventory. A finished plan can select the most useful pairing; across multiple plans, distribute secondary pairings by focus and avoid repeating them in every version. Incidental pants or other-item mentions do not turn that item into the target product; retain pairing advice and only keep another item's detail when it directly explains that pairing. Do not add an unrelated standalone specification of the other item.
14. **Finish a product topic before changing topics.** Keep each distinct fit, material, or other product topic together; move to the next topic only after the current explanation is complete. When a pain opening is used, put its related topic first.
15. **Ending pressure and dynamic data differ.** Put clear non-price/non-stock closing pressure at the end only when context establishes it as a close. A standalone price, discount, inventory, or scarcity cue is not a closing CTA and is not a required product highlight; default to omission unless the user wants it and the current fact is confirmed, then place it after the product explanation. If praise/product information and CTA share one subtitle ID, keep the whole cue at the end; never duplicate or split one ID across opening and ending. Mark any dynamic price/stock fact for confirmation and do not treat it as the reason to buy; if it cannot be confirmed for publication, exclude the whole cue from formal clip IDs and list its source ID and pending fact in the risk note.
16. **Resolve product conflicts with evidence.** Prefer reliable product information; if none settles the disagreement, choose the clearer and more usable passage and report the conflict. Distinct model/version measurements are not contradictions. Opposing subjective opinions can both remain with their conditions.
17. **Default plan count.** Produce one complete plan unless the user gives a count. If the user asks for multiple but gives no number, ask for the number. Never force extra plans with a weaker hook; pain may be the alternate plan when requested and supported.
18. **Coverage remains semantic.** Manually compare every selected distinct buying point with the final ordered highlight coverage list. A source-check pass does not prove the ledger contains every real highlight.
19. **Output metadata.** Preserve source wording and IDs; with a verified sentence-level edit, display only the retained sentence in the clip text and list the exact removed sentence separately as a source exception. Mark the in-cue cut point for replay unless actually checked. Count the full cue duration conservatively. Keep source sequence lists consistent with the selected plan. Titles/tags must match the chosen opening and cannot repeat a platform-flagged phrase, SKU, price, stock, or color-matching hook.
20. **Requested plans allocate highlights by importance.** Two requested plans may use different qualified openings or order. Reuse core buying reasons when useful; assign secondary styling, color, and use-case details to the plan where they fit, so a minor point need not repeat in every version. Record every source-supported highlight in the inventory and explain each plan's omission; a secondary point must appear in at least one plan unless the 120-second cap forces a documented cut from all suitable plans.
21. **Pain eligibility follows the customer, not the product category.** A clearly stated concern about wearing or using shoes, bags, scarves, or other goods can be a candidate. It still needs a clear customer source or host paraphrase; a bare product/color description is not a customer concern.
22. **The 1.2× runtime is capped at two minutes.** Each final plan must estimate to 120 seconds or less after rounding to milliseconds and counting known unaccelerated portions. Shorten repeated wording or lower-priority secondary details before rendering; the renderer must reject a plan over the cap.
23. **Honor the user's fixed material-phrase exclusion.** Treat “三醋酸羊绒羊毛” and spacing, punctuation, or inserted-“的” variants as a user-designated edit rule, not as a universal platform/legal finding. Remove the full sentence containing it; a separate clean sentence in the same cue may remain with a sentence-cut note. Never leave the phrase in selected transcript, title, or tags.
